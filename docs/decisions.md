# Decision log

Every judgment call in the project, its reason, and the alternative that was rejected. New rows are appended; superseded rows are marked, not deleted.

| ID | Decision | Choice | Why | Rejected alternative |
| :- | :- | :- | :- | :- |
| D01 | iPinYou period | Season 2 training, 6–12 Jun 2013 | All 5 advertisers ran the same week | Season 3: different dates per advertiser |
| D02 | Criteo revenue | Counted once per attributed conversion_id | Conversions repeat across impression rows | Summing cpo over impressions |
| D03 | Volume threshold | At least 30 clicks before comparing CTR; Wilson 95% intervals shown | At about 0.08% CTR, 1,000 impressions give under 1 click | A 1,000-impression threshold |
| D04 | Ratios | Sum ÷ sum at every level | Busy hours and big segments get their true weight | Average of row-level ratios |
| D05 | Cohort week 0 | Labelled "existing" | Data starts mid-flight, so week 0 mixes old and new campaigns | Treating week 0 as new advertisers |
| D06 | Partial week | Criteo days 28–30 excluded from weekly cuts (the data runs to 22:00 on day 30, just under 31 days) | A short week distorts weekly comparisons | Keeping a short week 4 |
| D07 | Bridge method | Log-mean (LMDI) decomposition | Exact and independent of factor order | Sequential substitution, which depends on order |
| D08 | Bridge timing | Revenue by conversion week; compare middle weeks | Edge weeks lose conversions to censoring at both ends | Revenue by impression week |
| D09 | Floor guardrail | Keep at least 90% of impressions per segment | Stops a revenue-maximising floor from starving advertisers | Unconstrained revenue maximum |
| D10 | ROAS | Revenue ÷ media cost, labelled as a proxy | No order value in the data | Calling the proxy ROAS |
| D11 | Fill rate | Win rate, labelled as a proxy | Bid log excludes requests iPinYou skipped | Calling it fill rate |
| D12 | Categories | Behavioural segments (Criteo) plus an illustrative industry cut (iPinYou) | cat1–cat9 are undisclosed; iPinYou has one advertiser per industry | Presenting either as true category analysis |
| D13 | Revenue date | Every Criteo revenue figure (Q9, Q10, Q15, dashboard) is booked by conversion_day | KPI cards and the Q15 waterfall must tie to the same total | Impression day in some tables, conversion day in others |
| D14 | Margin timing | Margin % shown for the whole period or the middle weeks only, labelled a booked-date view | Cost is dated by impression and revenue by conversion, so a daily margin compares mismatched periods and both window edges are distorted | A daily margin line |
| D15 | Floor selection | Split won auctions in half by hash(bid_id); choose each segment's floor on one half, report its lift on the other. If the Season 2 test logs (13–15 Jun) load with bid and paying price, add an out-of-time check | Scoring on the same data overstates the lift; a 6–9 vs 10–12 Jun split puts Thu–Sun against Mon–Wed, so weekday effects look like error | In-sample maximum; a date split |
| D16 | Floor segment volume | At least 10,000 won impressions per segment in the fit half | Small segments give noisy floor choices | No volume rule |
| D17 | Campaign volume (Q11, Q16) | At least 50 attributed conversions: the highest tested threshold whose campaigns still cover ≥ 90% of revenue (390 of 675 campaigns, 91.8% of revenue, set in the data profile). The rest are grouped as "long tail" | Conversions are skewed, so a fixed threshold chosen in advance could drop the median campaign or keep noisy ones | A fixed threshold chosen in advance |
| D18 | Time patterns (iPinYou) | Read by hour and date, not weekday | Seven days give one observation per weekday | Hour × weekday heatmap |
| D19 | Anomaly checks (Q8) | Criteo's 30 days of impression-dated volume and media cost; iPinYou hourly version kept as a short demo | Six same-hour comparisons are too few; conversion-dated revenue would flag censored edge weeks as anomalies | iPinYou z-scores only |
| D20 | Data sources | iPinYou from the Figshare mirror of Season 2, verified against the archive's own files.md5; Criteo from Criteo's official Hugging Face repo, pinned to one commit and checked by SHA-256 | The original UCL and go.criteo.net links are dead | Unverified mirrors |
| D21 | Raw layer | Parquet with every column stored as text; types applied in staging | A malformed value fails visibly in staging instead of silently becoming NULL at load | Type inference at load |
| D22 | bz2 handling | Decompress one log at a time to a temporary file, convert it to Parquet, delete the temporary file | Decompressed bid logs are about 13 GB against about 22 GB of free disk | Decompressing everything up front |
| D23 | iPinYou bid prices | Treat bid_price as a fixed, deliberately high test bid, not a real market bid. Paying price (the second-highest bid) remains the market-price signal. State this wherever Q5–Q7 are presented | The dataset README says the campaigns ran "with a fixed relatively high-price bidding strategy … for the purpose of getting enough impressions and their paying prices". Bid headroom (Q5) is therefore inflated, and the floor simulation (Q7) keeps more impressions than real bids would | Reading bid headroom as real willingness to pay |
| D24 | Criteo cpo | cpo is filled on every row (a campaign-level cost per order), so revenue relies on the attributed = 1 filter, and no data test expects cpo to be empty on unattributed rows. How cpo is chosen within a conversion is covered by D28 | Confirmed from the raw file on 2026-10-25: non-converting rows carry a cpo value | A test expecting cpo only on attributed rows, which would fail by design |
| D25 | iPinYou lookups | city.en.txt's first line ("0 unknown") is space-separated; lookup files are parsed leniently in Python, logs strictly in DuckDB | One malformed line in a 362-line lookup should not block the build; logs keep strict parsing | Hand-editing the source file |
| D26 | iPinYou log parsing | Fixed dialect, no sniffing: tab-delimited, no quotes, escapes or comments. Rows missing only their last field (user_profile_ids) keep a NULL there; any other field-count error, or a stray carriage return, fails the build. Parsed rows are reconciled to the file's line count | Real rows include user agents with raw control bytes (e.g. \x01) and a conversion row with no trailing tab; automatic dialect detection rejected both | Skipping malformed rows (silently loses data); sniffed CSV parsing |
| D27 | Duplicate impression logs | stg_ipy_imps and fact_auction hold one row per won auction (first logged event and its price). impression_events and pay_price_events_sum keep the raw-row counts, used only to reproduce the paper's Table 3 | 45,044 auctions (0.4%) appear 2–24 times in the impression log, mostly seconds apart with identical prices; an auction can be won only once, and keeping copies broke fact_auction's one-row-per-bid grain | Counting every logged row as an impression (the paper's convention); dropping the duplicates without a trace |
| D28 | Criteo revenue per conversion | fact_conversion grain is conversion_id × campaign_id; revenue = cpo on the last impression before the conversion, for attributed rows | cpo varies across a conversion's impression rows in 28% of conversions, even within one campaign, so MAX(cpo) would overstate revenue; 2,859 conversion_ids (0.66%) span 2–3 campaigns with different conversion times, so they behave as separate conversions | MAX(cpo) per conversion_id; averaging cpo across the path |
| D29 | Units and benchmark conventions | Prices are CPM in Chinese fen (Zhang et al., 2014, §2.2); one impression costs pay_price / 1000 fen. The Table 3 reconciliation counts impression, click and conversion log rows, duplicates included, as the paper's pipeline does; the analysis counts auctions. The paper truncates cost and win ratio and rounds CTR, CVR, CPM and eCPC; gate 2 applies the same convention and requires all 50 cells to match exactly | With these conventions all five advertisers match Table 3 exactly on every published cell; counting distinct clicked auctions gives −9 to +9 click differences. Rounding everything leaves 3 cells one digit off (e.g. 3386's win ratio is 20.2087%, printed 20.20); truncating everything leaves 8 | Treating prices as RMB (the dataset specification's wording); reconciling on deduplicated counts; a 0.1% tolerance, which would let cost drift by about 200 fen unnoticed |
| D30 | Slot attributes | Size, visibility and format are stored per auction on fact_auction; dim_slot keeps slot-level context only. Visibility codes are labelled per the specification (1 above the fold, 2 below, 0 unknown); 255 and the format codes are undocumented and kept as codes | Visibility varies between impressions on 28,501 of 28,599 unstable slots, so it describes the impression, not the slot | A modal value per slot, which would misstate 37% of bids |
| D31 | Clicks and conversions without an impression | clicked and converted require a won auction; the 87 clicks and 13 conversions without an impression row are flagged and excluded from CTR and CVR. Gate 1 allows up to 1.5% of clicks and 5% of conversions | Every one has a bid, and they cluster in the 22:00 hour of 10 Jun, when bid volume jumped fivefold: an impression-logging gap | Counting them, which would put clicks on auctions that were never won |
| D32 | Mixed attribution within a conversion | A conversion × campaign is attributed if any of its rows says so; gate 1 fails if mixed pairs exceed 0.01% | Only 26 of 438,679 pairs (0.006%) are mixed | Dropping those conversions |
| D33 | Paying price above the bid | Kept as recorded; gate 2 bounds it (≤ 5% of each advertiser's wins, none for 1458 and 3386, excess ≤ 50 fen). Bid headroom (Q5) floors negative values at zero and states the caveat | 1.55% of won auctions, only for 3358, 3427 and 3476 at a few fixed bid prices, so the logged bid is not the bid the exchange cleared against | Dropping the rows, which would bias win rate and spend |
| D34 | Unnamed regions and cities | dim_geo keeps NULL names and adds region_label / city_label falling back to the id | The lookup files miss ids covering 10.8% of bids (cities) and < 0.01% (regions); no planned question depends on geography | Dropping unnamed locations |
| D35 | Metric layer | Each ratio is a DuckDB macro over sums (`ctr(clicks, impressions)`); views at fixed grains carry the additive base columns beside the ratios; the dashboard and sql/05_analysis read only these. Gate 2 ties every view back to its mart | One definition per KPI, and sum ÷ sum holds at any grain, because callers pass sums and can roll a view up from its base columns | Ratio columns precomputed in the marts, which cannot be re-aggregated and get redefined in the BI tool |
| D36 | Criteo reporting windows | Whole period = impression days 0–30 plus every conversion they produced (conversion days 0–60). Middle weeks = days 7–27 (weeks 1–3) on both cost and revenue | No conversion lags beyond 30 days (max 29.99), so the whole-period P&L is complete; 18.6% of its revenue books on days 31–60. Conversion-dated revenue on day d holds about P(lag ≤ d) of steady state, measured on attributed revenue with whole-day lags: 82% on day 7, 90% on day 14, 95% on day 21 (weekly averages 86%, 92%, 97% for weeks 1–3; see D40) | Cutting revenue at day 30, which drops a fifth of the revenue those impressions earned and understates margin |
| D37 | Attribution rate over time | Shown by week, labelled as mix-affected; Q14 and Q15 control for conversion lag before reading a trend | Attributed share is 77% for conversions within a day of the last impression and 24% at 14–30 days. Early conversion weeks miss long-lag conversions, so the weekly rate falls from 70% (week 0) to 55% (week 3) with no change in behaviour | Reading the decline as growing revenue leakage |
| D38 | Criteo CTR | Reported as a within-Criteo comparison only (campaigns, weeks); never set against iPinYou or industry CTRs | CTR is 36%, far above display norms; Criteo's README says the data "has been sub-sampled and anonymized" without giving the method. Every conversion row has click = 1, so CVR is a post-click rate | Treating 36% as a real click-through rate |
| D39 | New and churned campaigns | Calendar weeks: new in week w = first impression in w (blank for week 0, D05); churned in w = impressions in w − 1, none in w, shown for weeks 1–3 only | Lines up with weekly reporting and needs a complete current and prior week | A rolling 7-day test as of every day, which gives the same answer at week ends but no weekly series |
| D40 | Weekly revenue comparisons | Week-over-week revenue changes (Q15, dashboard deltas) are shown raw and lag-adjusted, dividing each week's revenue by its completeness (86%, 92%, 97% for weeks 1–3, from the attributed-revenue lag distribution) | Revenue is left-censored inside the middle weeks: raw growth overstates demand by about 8 points from week 1 to 2 and 5 points from week 2 to 3, enough to dominate a real change | Comparing raw weekly revenue, which reads censoring as growth; dropping week 1, which leaves a single comparison |
