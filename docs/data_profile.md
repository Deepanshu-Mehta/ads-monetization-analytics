# Data profile

Generated by `scripts/profile_data.py` from the built warehouse (`python run_all.py --profile`).
Every number below is queried, not typed. Decisions referenced as D-numbers are in
[`decisions.md`](decisions.md).

## Summary

Both datasets are fit for the planned analysis. The iPinYou build **reproduces the benchmark
paper's Table 3 exactly** for all five Season 2 advertisers, and all 14 quality-gate tests pass
on every build. The findings that shape the analysis:

1. **Bids are fixed test bids, not market bids (D23).** iPinYou ran these campaigns at a fixed,
   deliberately high bid. Paying price is the market signal; bid headroom (Q5) and the floor
   simulation (Q7) must carry this caveat.
2. **189,179 won auctions (1.55%) record a paying
   price above the bid (D33)**, all for three advertisers at a few fixed bid prices: an artefact of
   how bids were logged, bounded by a gate-2 test.
3. **45,044 auctions were logged more than once (D27).** They are one auction each in
   the warehouse; raw-row counts are kept only to reproduce the paper.
4. **Criteo's cpo varies within a conversion** in 27.5% of conversions, so revenue
   uses the cpo on the last impression before the conversion (D28).
5. **Criteo holds about 436K conversions, not the 45K its README states.**

## Minimum-volume rules

**Rates (CTR, CVR) need at least 30 events (D03).** At iPinYou's CTR of about
0.072%, 30 clicks need roughly
41,791 won impressions. Segments below the
threshold show as "low volume", and every reported rate carries a Wilson 95% interval. How much
of the data each cut keeps:

| cut | segments | segments with ≥ 30 clicks | won impressions covered % |
| :- | -: | -: | -: |
| ad slot | 336,338 | 42 | 33.2 |
| advertiser × hour | 120 | 81 | 90.1 |
| size × visibility | 39 | 22 | 95.3 |
| advertiser × exchange | 15 | 15 | 100.0 |

Only a handful of individual slots reach 30 clicks, so slot-level CTR is not reliable. Inventory
questions (Q3) are cut by size × visibility instead, which keeps most impressions above the threshold.

**Campaigns need enough attributed conversions for margin to mean something (D17).** The rule is
the highest threshold whose qualifying campaigns still cover at least 90% of revenue:

| min attributed conversions | campaigns kept | revenue covered % |
| :- | -: | -: |
| 1 | 675 | 100.0 |
| 5 | 662 | 100.0 |
| 10 | 640 | 99.8 |
| 20 | 565 | 98.3 |
| 30 | 488 | 96.2 |
| 50 | 390 | 91.8 |
| 100 | 259 | 83.0 |

**Chosen: at least 50 attributed conversions**, keeping 390 of
675 campaigns and 91.8% of revenue. The rest are grouped as "long tail" in Q11 and Q16.

## Analysis windows

| Dataset | Window | Notes |
| :- | :- | :- |
| iPinYou | 6–12 Jun 2013 (Season 2 training) | Seven days, so one observation per weekday: read time patterns by hour and date, not weekday (D18). The 22:00 hour on 10 Jun carries 1,119,944 bids against a median of 253,584 per hour. |
| Criteo impressions | Days 0–30 | Day 30 ends at 21:00, so the data spans just under 31 days, not 30. Weekly cuts use weeks 0–3 (days 0–27); days 28–30 form a partial week and are excluded from weekly comparisons (D06). |
| Criteo conversions | Days 0–60 | Conversions are tracked for 30 days after each impression. Revenue is booked by conversion day; edge weeks are censored, so week-over-week revenue compares middle weeks only (D08, D13). |
